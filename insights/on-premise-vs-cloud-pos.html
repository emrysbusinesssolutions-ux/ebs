On-premise vs cloud POS: data ownership compared

Both models can run a shop. They differ in who holds the data, what happens in an outage, how you pay, and how you leave. This is a practical comparison, not a verdict.

Book a demo See pricing

On-premise data · Offline-first · Delivered remotely

The short comparison

  • Cloud: the vendor runs the servers; you reach the system through a browser; you usually pay per month per user; internet is required for every action.
  • On-premise: the system runs on your computer; you reach it over your local network; you pay for the software and support; internet is optional for daily work.
  • Hybrid: on-premise operation with optional cloud sync for reporting or a second location.

Where the data lives

In a cloud POS, the database sits in the vendor's environment. You can usually export reports, but the live system is not something you can copy to a USB drive at closing time. In an on-premise system the database file is on your machine: you can take a copy, keep it after the contract ends, and hand it to an accountant without asking permission.

That is the real difference behind the word "ownership" — not pride, but exit. Ask each vendor: what exactly do I get if I leave, and how long does it take?

Outages and internet quality

Cloud systems depend on the connection between your counter and the vendor's servers. On-premise systems depend on your local network and power. Neither is magic, but the failure modes are different: a cloud outage is outside your control; a local issue is something you can see, test and fix.

If your area has frequent outages, test a demo under those conditions before signing. Unplug the internet and try to complete a sale.

Cost over time

Cloud pricing is usually predictable per month, and the vendor handles servers, backups and upgrades. On-premise pricing often has a setup cost and a monthly platform/support fee, and you own the hardware and the database. Compare three years, not the first invoice: hardware, UPS, support, migration and the cost of leaving all belong in the comparison.

Backups and responsibility

In the cloud, backups are the vendor's job — check the retention and restore process anyway. On-premise, backups are shared: the software can automate them, but someone has to verify that a restore works. A backup you have never restored is a hope, not a backup.

When on-premise fits

  • The internet is unreliable, or the shop cannot stop when it fails.
  • You want the database file in your own hands, and a clean exit path.
  • You already have a computer and a local network that can host the system.
  • You prefer a fixed setup plus support over per-user subscriptions.

When cloud fits

  • The team works from several cities and needs one live dashboard.
  • You do not want to maintain a server, UPS or backups.
  • Internet is reliable and the vendor's uptime is proven.
  • Per-user monthly pricing suits your cash flow better than setup costs.

Questions to ask either vendor

  • What still works when the internet is down?
  • Where is the database, and can I get a full copy?
  • How are backups taken, kept and restored — and who tests restores?
  • What happens to my data and access if I stop paying?
  • What does support cost after year one?

Frequently asked

Is on-premise more expensive than cloud?

It depends on hardware, support and how long you keep the system. Compare three-year totals, including migration and the cost of leaving, rather than the first monthly invoice.

Can we access an on-premise system from another city?

Daily operations run on the local network. Remote access is possible when configured, but it is a separate decision from offline-first operation.

Who is responsible for backups?

With EBS, backups are automated and the database file is yours; verifying restores is a joint responsibility agreed in the Service Agreement.
