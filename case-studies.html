The challenge

Mixed units, credit, and stock in more than one place.

The business buys and sells rice by the bag, pyi, viss and kilogram. Stock moves between branches, and much of the trade runs on credit: a customer takes goods today and pays later.

When those records live in notebooks, chat messages and memory, the owner cannot see the day's position without adding it up by hand. A missed entry becomes an argument at the end of the month, and a conversion mistake follows the stock through every report.

  • One product sold in several units, each with its own price
  • Credit sales across many customers, paid in parts
  • Stock held in more than one location
  • Cash counted at the end of a trading session
  • Changes that need a traceable record, not a memory

Three questions, every day

A trading business needs one answer to each: what is in stock, who owes what, and what did today sell? When the answers come from different books, the owner spends the evening reconciling them.

  • What is in stock — and in which unit?
  • Who owes what — and since when?
  • What did the day sell — in cash and credit?

The solution

The same system EBS sells, running the business.

The deployment is not a demo environment. It is an on-premise installation supporting counters, stock, credit and reporting every trading day.

Point of sale

Multi-unit selling by bag, pyi, viss and kilogram. The cashier sells in the customer's unit while the stock ledger records the base unit.

58 mm receipts

A thermal receipt for the customer, and an A4 invoice or PDF copy for the file. Every document comes from the same sale record.

Inventory & stock ledger

Purchases, sales, transfers and adjustments post to one ledger. Low stock is visible before it runs out, not after.

Receivables

Customer ledgers, statements and payment-in records, so credit sales and repayments stay in one place instead of a separate book.

Multi-branch transfers

Stock moves between branches through recorded transfers, with per-branch permission gates deciding who can send and receive.

Cash sessions

Each branch opens and closes its own cash session, so the day's cash is counted against the day's recorded sales.

Roles & audit log

A role × module × action permission matrix, plus an audit log of changes that records who did what and when.

Telegram bot

Daily figures, stock and debts arrive in the owner's Telegram chat, instead of a phone call to the shop or a late-night spreadsheet.

Offline sync

The app shell and local database keep the counter working through network drops; an outbox syncs when the connection returns.

What it proves

Tested by a trading day, not a demo script.

Most software claims are demonstrated once. This deployment is exercised every day the business opens, which makes it the most honest evidence EBS can publish.

It does not prove that the system fits every business. It does prove that it can run a rice trading operation with mixed units, credit and multiple branches — and that the features EBS sells are the features it uses.

  • In daily production, not a pilot project
  • Built around the local trading model: units, credit, branches
  • The product sold to customers is the product EBS runs
  • Upgrades and support are exercised by a real business first

Baseline form

Before go-live, record how the process works today: units, documents, closing routine, time spent and the reports the owner actually uses. No results are promised at this stage.

02

Measured results

After an agreed period, compare against the baseline using reports exported from the system. Every figure has a source transaction behind it, so it can be checked.

Customer approval

The customer reviews the draft and approves every name, figure and quote. They can request changes or anonymity before anything is published.
